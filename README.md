## From systems administration to systems architecture

I’m interested in the decisions behind a working system: its dependencies, access boundaries, maintenance needs and recovery options. I value designs that are understandable to the people who will run them, with clear trade-offs around security, performance and complexity.

## Automation with an operational purpose

I use PowerShell, Ansible, AWX and shell scripting to explore ways to reduce repetition and make administration more consistent. I also build small tools around specific operational needs.

The failure path matters as much as the successful one: useful status, clear logs, explicit inputs, sensible safeguards and a recovery plan. Getting a service running is a starting point; maintaining and recovering it deserve the same attention.

## A place to test ideas

Outside my professional role, my homelab gives me room to explore Linux, Docker Swarm and Compose, self-hosted services, identity and secure remote access. I use it to connect components, challenge assumptions and learn how designs behave in practice.

That includes monitoring and observability with Prometheus, Grafana and Uptime Kuma, alongside workflow automation, backups and recovery. These personal projects support my learning and provide material for practical explanations.
